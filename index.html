--- layout: single title: About permalink: / author_profile: true classes: wide --- I am a trained computational linguist and cognitive scientist working on reference production within the Corpus-based typology group at the Institute for the Interdisciplinary Study of Language Evolution, University of Zurich, under supervision of dr. Stefan Schnell. Coming from a background in computational linguistics and cognitive science, I study language as a computational system shaped by different communicative and cognitive pressures. In my research, I primarily make use of computational models to formalize the parameters and conditions under which linguistic regularities emerge or change when using language. I currently focus on the mechanisms behind reference production, taking a comparative perspective.

PhD research

I did my PhD at Tilburg University under primary supervision of prof. dr. Max M. Louwerse and secondary supervision of dr. Peter Hendrix. I studied different regularities in language use, through computational models. I took a cognitive science approach, which manifests itself in two primary ways. First, simplification was driving factor in my research: It is a necessary and important aspect of computational modeling to understand and translate information processing to cognitive processes. The drive for simplicity is moreover deeply rooted in the human language system and cognition more generally, and is as such a guiding principle in theories of cognition, thus aligning exceptionally well with the computational modeling approach. And second, I focus primarily on spoken dialog, the most fundamental form of language use which operates most directly on our cognition. At Tilburg University, I worked on a project called Virtual Humans in the Brabant Economy ViBE (PI Prof. dr. Max M. Louwerse), where I investigated spoken dialog with the goal of improving the language capabilities an embodied conversational agent.
